## Design Context

### Users

Miami Beach Fire Department Emergency Operations Center personnel use this
private dashboard as a common operating picture during routine monitoring and
high-pressure incidents. Primary contexts are a 1920x1080 operations display,
desktop and laptop workstations, and touch-capable landscape tablets; phone
access is a secondary but fully supported quick-check context. Users need to
scan source-backed conditions quickly, open a record or map feature for exact
provenance, and distinguish current, stale, unavailable, authoritative,
advisory, and supplemental information without interpretation.

### Brand Personality

Authoritative, calm, and operational. The interface should inspire confidence
and urgency without alarmism. It uses the canonical Miami Beach Fire Department
identity and concise emergency-management language; it must never simulate
unsupported incident, facility, infrastructure, or system status.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light, dense, Microsoft/Fluent-inspired command dashboard aligned with the
supplied Miami Beach EOC visual reference. The visual hierarchy is led by the
operational map, followed by compact KPIs and clearly grouped source-backed
panels. Navy-tinted neutrals, Miami Beach blue, and restrained semantic
red/amber/green accents should feel municipal and trustworthy rather than
consumer-app playful. Avoid decorative gradients, glassmorphism, glowing dark
themes, generic card galleries, oversized hero metrics, and visual effects that
compete with operational content.

### Design Principles

1. Truth before polish: label authority, provenance, observation time, stale
   state, and unavailable data explicitly; never fabricate a reassuring status.
2. Map-first scanability: preserve geographic context and make every visible
   feature keyboard-, pointer-, and touch-selectable with a fast, legible detail
   surface.
3. Dense without collision: use deliberate grid tracks, bounded text, and
   progressive disclosure so cards never overlap and critical labels never
   truncate ambiguously.
4. Adapt by context: fit the full 1920x1080 command view without unintended page
   scrolling, use a two-column tablet layout, and retain all critical functions
   in a one-column phone flow with at least 44x44px touch targets.
5. Accessible under pressure: meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard
   expectations, preserve visible focus and reduced-motion behavior, and prefer
   immediate skeleton/summary feedback over blocking loaders.
